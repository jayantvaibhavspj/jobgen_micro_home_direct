Modular and prefab homes: what sets them apart?

Understand the language of off-site building, then compare the details that matter for your site and plans.

A practical guide from Micro Homes Direct

01 · KNOW THE TERMS

Prefab is the broad idea; modular describes one approach

“Prefabricated” generally means that some or most of a building is made away from its final site before it is transported for installation. It describes where parts of the building are produced, rather than one specific design or construction system.

“Modular” usually refers to a building assembled from sections or modules made off-site. Depending on the product, modules may arrive largely complete or need further work after placement. In everyday conversation the terms overlap, so the label alone cannot tell you exactly what is included.

Compare the product, not just the label

Ask what is manufactured off-site, what work remains after delivery, and which items are included in the quoted specification.

02 · COMPARE WITH CONFIDENCE

Questions that make options easier to compare

  1. What will arrive on the truck?

    Confirm dimensions, layout, fixtures, services preparation and finish level. Ask which items are optional or completed on site.

  2. How will it reach and fit your site?

    Check transport dimensions, route access, unloading equipment and the space needed for placement. A supplier can explain product requirements; a site professional can assess local conditions.

  3. What needs to happen before use?

    Ask about foundations, drainage, utility connections and any site works. Requirements depend on the property and intended use.

  4. Which local rules apply?

    Planning and building requirements vary. Confirm with your local council and appropriately qualified professionals before committing to a design or delivery date.
