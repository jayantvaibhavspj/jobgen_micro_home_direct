01 · START WITH THE WHOLE PROJECT

Start with the total project, not just the home

The price of the building is only one part of your budget. Your final spend depends on the model and fit-out you choose, where it needs to go, what the site requires and which approvals or services apply to your plans.

Before comparing prices, decide what “ready to use” means for you. A building delivered to a prepared site is different from a complete project that also includes foundations, connections and any work needed to meet local requirements.

A useful starting point

Project total = home and selected options + delivery and placement + site works + services + approvals and professional advice + contingency.

The main costs to allow for

  1. 1. The home and its fit-out

    Confirm the exact model, dimensions, layout and included fixtures. Ask for options to be itemised, including kitchen or bathroom fit-outs, insulation, windows, electrical items and any requested changes. Check whether the quoted price includes GST and ask which items are excluded.

  2. 2. Transport and placement

    Delivery depends on the destination, route, access and the equipment needed to unload or position the building. Share site photos, driveway width, overhead obstructions and the proposed set-down area. Ask whether transport, permits for the journey, crane hire and placement are included or quoted separately.

  3. 3. Site preparation and foundations

    The site may need clearing, levelling, drainage, retaining work or a suitable foundation. The right solution depends on ground conditions, slope, access and the building requirements. Have the site assessed before treating a preliminary estimate as a fixed cost.

  4. 4. Utilities and connections

    Power, water, wastewater and data requirements vary with the product and how you plan to use it. Confirm what connections the building is prepared for, then ask licensed local trades to assess the route, capacity and connection work at your property.

  5. 5. Planning, approvals and advice

    Requirements can differ between councils and depend on the property, intended use and design. Contact your local council or a qualified building professional before ordering. Allow for any application fees, reports, inspections or professional services that apply to your project.

  6. 6. Ongoing and financing costs

    If you are borrowing, compare the lender’s full fees, interest and repayment terms rather than relying on an indicative repayment alone. Also consider insurance, maintenance and any ongoing site or service costs relevant to your use.

How to get a useful, comparable quote

Give each supplier the same brief so you can compare like for like. Include your intended use, preferred model and options, delivery suburb, site access details and whether you need help understanding what is required before installation.

  • Request an itemised quote with GST status and validity date.
  • Ask for inclusions and exclusions in writing.
  • Confirm delivery, unloading, placement and any access assumptions.
  • Check payment milestones, lead-time assumptions and cancellation terms.
  • Ask what site information, approvals or services you need to arrange.
  • Get site-specific costs from the relevant trades and professionals.

A low headline price is not necessarily the lowest complete project cost. Compare the same specification and make a separate allowance for anything that has not been confirmed.
