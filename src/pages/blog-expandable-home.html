SPACE & PLANNING · SITE GUIDE

Planning an expandable home for your site.

A flexible layout works best when the building, access route and site are considered together.

A practical guide from Micro Homes Direct

01 · PLAN THE WHOLE SITE

Begin with how the space will be used

List the rooms and activities you need, who will use them and how the home should connect to the surrounding area. Think about privacy, daylight, storage, circulation and the relationship between indoor and outdoor spaces.

Then compare available layouts against the usable dimensions, not just the advertised footprint. Check door and window positions, room sizes, service locations and how any expansion changes the final arrangement. Product configurations and inclusions vary, so confirm the exact plan for the model you are considering.

Keep a simple site brief

Note intended use, preferred layout, delivery address, access constraints, utility needs and the information still to be confirmed.

02 · CHECK ACCESS & SERVICES

The practical details to confirm early

  1. Delivery route and set-down area

    Check road access, turning space, driveway width, gates, overhead branches or wires, slopes and ground conditions. Ask what vehicle and unloading equipment the delivery requires.

  2. Placement and foundations

    Confirm the product’s support requirements and have the site assessed for levels, drainage and ground conditions. A qualified local professional can advise on suitable site preparation.

  3. Connections and intended use

    Ask which electrical, water and wastewater provisions are included or prepared. Have licensed local trades check the connection requirements for your property and intended use.

  4. Approvals and local advice

    Rules depend on location, property and use. Check with your local council and relevant qualified professionals before ordering; do not assume a particular approval outcome.

03 · GET A USEFUL QUOTE

Give suppliers the same information

For a clearer comparison, share your preferred model and options, destination, site access details and any known requirements. Ask for inclusions and exclusions, delivery assumptions, placement responsibilities and the validity period in writing.

Keep product pricing separate from site works, utility connections, professional advice and other costs that need local assessment. Confirm each item before treating an estimate as a complete project budget.
